Gap questions

1. Access gap
  • "If you wanted to stop working at [target retirement age], what money could you actually use between then and 59½? [↑]"
  • Label: "So the money you've saved is behind a door that doesn't open until you're almost 60."
2. Crash gap
  • "What happened to your 401(k) in 2022? [↑]"
  • "How would you feel if the market dropped 30% the year before you wanted to stop working? [↑]"
  • Label: "So the money you're counting on can drop right when you need it most."
3. Tax gap
  • "Do you know what tax bracket you'll be in when you pull that money out? [↑]"
  • "Do you think taxes will be higher or lower in 20 years? [↑]"
  • Label: "So the IRS is a partner in your 401(k), and they decide later how much of it you keep."
4. Body gap
  • "Honestly, how many more years can your body do this job? [↑]"
  • "If you got hurt tomorrow and couldn't work, what happens to the bills? [↑]"
  • "What coverage do you have outside of work? [↑]"
  • Label: "So the machine making all the money isn't protected, and the coverage you do have goes away if you leave the job."
5. Overtime gap
  • "Last year with overtime, what did you make? [↑]" "How much of that is still sitting somewhere working for you? [↑]"
  • Label: "So you make good money, but none of it is growing with purpose."
6. Waiting gap
  • "What's kept you from setting this up before now? [↑]"
  • "If you waited five more years, what do you think that costs you? [↑]"
  • Label: "So every year this waits, you lose growth years and you bet that your health and your body hold up."

Part 6The five specific objections

1. Time: "I'm too busy" / "Now's not a good time"
  • Flip the sequence: "Waiting until you have more money to start saving is like waiting until you're healthy to go to the doctor. [/] Do you think it gets easier at 40 with the kids in travel ball? [↑]"
  • Busy is the best time: "Do you think you'll ever be busy again after this? [↑] Then it's better to set it up now. The draft runs on its own, so the next crazy month doesn't stop it."
  • Younger self: "If you could talk to 20-year-old you on his first day on the job, and he said 'I'm too busy,' what would you tell him? [↑]"
  • Trade from the bottom: "Of everything you spend on every month, what's the least important? [↑] Would you trade that for this? [↑]"
  • Cost of waiting: "Insurance is priced on age and health. Every birthday the coverage costs more, and you lose a year of growth. [/] Does waiting make this cheaper or more expensive? [↑]"
2. Money: "It's too much"
  • It's good that it's a lot: "Honestly, that's a good thing. It means you'll take it seriously, and the people who take it seriously get the best results."
  • Spent either way: "That [monthly contribution] is getting spent somewhere either way: the truck note, DoorDash, whatever. [/] Does it go somewhere that gets you closer to [target retirement age] or further away? [↑]"
  • If all it does: "If all this did was protect your family with [death benefit] and make sure a crash can never take back your credited gains, would it be worth [monthly contribution] a month? [↑]"
  • The free test: "If this plan were free, would you take it? [↑]" → "Of course." → "So it's not the plan, it's the number. [/] Let's look at the number."
  • "Can you do it for less?" "Yes, you decide what goes in. [/] At [monthly contribution] you're on track for [target retirement age]. At [lower contribution] it's closer to [later age from the illustration]. [//] Which version do you want? [↑]"
  • "Term is cheaper." "If they were the same price, which would you want: coverage that ends at [term end age] with nothing back, or coverage that builds cash you can use at [target retirement age]? [↑] That's why they're not the same price."
  • They truly can't afford it: "Let's find a number that works. What could you set aside every month without it touching the bills? [↑]"
3. Decision maker: "I need to talk to my wife"
  1. "What if she says no? [↑]" If they say "I'd probably still do it," then: "Awesome. Let's get you started."
  2. "What specifically do you think she'd disagree with? [↑]"
  3. "Does she know you're not happy with where things are for retirement? [↑] Is she good with you working until 65? [↑]" → "No." → "Then why do you think she'd be against something that fixes it? [↑]"
  4. Ask for support, not permission: "Does she usually back you on things that really matter to you? [↑] Is this one of those? [↑]"

One-call move: "Can we call her right now? [↑] I'll take five minutes and walk her through it."

4. Fit: "I don't like [this part]"

"Is [that part] more important to you than [their goal]? [↑] Because you can't keep everything exactly the same and get a different result."

IUL-specific fit objections

  • "I already have a 401(k)." "Love that. Keep it, especially the match. That's free money. [/] Quick question: can you touch it at [target retirement age] without a penalty? [↑] So what pays for [target retirement age] to 59½? [↑]"
  • "Dave Ramsey says buy term and invest the difference." "Term is great at what it does. Quick question: how's the 'invest the difference' part going so far? [↑] And what happens when the term ends at [term end age]? [↑]"
  • "I heard IULs are a scam" / "The fees are high." "Smart to be skeptical. You're right, there are charges, like every financial product, your 401(k) included. The ones that go bad are usually underfunded or sold off an unrealistic illustration. [/] Want me to show you exactly where the charges are on yours? [↑]"
  • "I don't want my money locked up." "Fair. In the first [surrender period] years there are surrender charges if you cancel, so this is for long-term money. You can still typically borrow against your available cash value. [/] When can you touch your 401(k)? [↑]"
  • "I'd rather just invest in the market." "Got it. How did 2022 feel? [↑] This isn't instead of the market for everything. It's the part of your money where a crash means a zero credit, not a loss."
5. Stall: "I need to think about it"
  • Past hesitation: "Do you think hesitating like this before is part of why you're [age] with [savings balance] saved? [↑]"
  • Burned you twice (a bad past policy or agent): "Totally fair. Did you marry the first person you ever dated? [↑] If one bad agent keeps you from the right plan now, you let him burn you twice."
  • Three decisions: "Has anyone ever walked you through how to make a decision like this? [↑] Mind if I do? It's three things. Do you like the plan? Do you like the company? Do you like me? [↑]" Three yeses: "Let's get you started."
  • Direction, not perfection: "If you hang up today without this, are you closer to [target retirement age] or further away? [↑]"
  • Information, not time: "You want to make an informed decision, right? [↑] Time won't give you more information. What information do you feel like you're missing? [↑]"
  • Test drive: "The only way to know for sure is to read the actual policy. That's what the free look is for. Let's get it approved and you can read it with it in your hands."
  • Sooner or later: "Are you going to set something up eventually? [↑] Then sooner beats later. The growth clock starts when you do."
  • Play it out: "Five years from now, nothing's changed. You're [age + 5], still working [hours] hours a week, money still locked in the [401(k)/IRA], body hurting a little more. How do you feel? [↑]" [///] "If nothing changes, nothing changes."
